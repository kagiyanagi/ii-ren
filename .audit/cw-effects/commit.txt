refactor(cw-effects): a shadow that stops recompiling itself, and a tint that was always red

Eight effect wrappers, 234 lines, 122 shadow callers between two of them. None
of them is interactive and none can be empty, loading or errored, so DESIGN.md
3.1's states and the brief's edge states do not apply here; what does apply is
section 8's budget, and this family is where that rule is enforced or lost,
since these are the widgets a delegate nests.

`StyledDropShadow` derived `samples: radius * 2 + 1`. Qt's own docs: *"This
property is not intended to be animated. Changing this property will cause the
underlying OpenGL shaders to be recompiled."* `DockAppButton` animates `radius`
5 → 10 on hover, so every dock hover recompiled the blur shader every frame —
in the one caller of the 51 that animates it. `samples` now hangs off a
`readonly` blur size instead, and because that size is `elevationMargin * 0.8`
= 8 and `ceil(8) * 2 + 1` = 17, the other 50 callers render exactly what they
rendered before, literals and all. Measured on Iris Xe at 1 and 8 shadows, two
repeats: 4 of 4 paired runs favour the fix by 26-69% frames. That also clears
the family's one `check-design.py` hit, the literal `radius: 8`.

`Colorizer` set `colorization: 1` and never set `colorizationColor`, whose
MultiEffect default is opaque red; `sourceColor` only fed `brightness`, and the
`Behavior on colorizationColor` animated a property nothing wrote. Every caller
would have come out red, which is presumably why it has none. One line.

`StyledBlurEffect` defaulted `source` to `wallpaper`, an id that does not exist
in its own scope — the family's only real qmllint warning. All four callers use
it as a `layer.effect` and set `source` themselves, which is the contract; the
dangling default is gone.

The two shadow wrappers do not merge. `RectangularShadow` is analytic and never
samples its target, which is why section 8 calls it cheap; `DropShadow` blurs
the target's own alpha, which is the only way to shadow an arc, a polygon or a
glyph — and the clock and cookie widgets among its 51 callers are exactly that.
DESIGN.md 6.2 already says as much. Both files now say which to reach for.

Rejected after measuring, in full in `.audit/cw-effects/notes.md`:

- `blurMax: 100` → 64, which DESIGN.md 8 names as the shell's most expensive
  value. A structural probe counts 11 internal blur items for `blurMax` 8/16/32
  and 13 for 64/100/128 — the extra level appears above 32, not above 64, so
  that change costs blur radius and saves no pass. 32 is the value that saves
  one, and it is a look judgement on a fullscreen surface, not a refactor.
- `cached: true` on `StyledDropShadow`: 144 shadowed tiles hold 60fps with and
  without it, static or animating.
- `verticalOffset: 1` to match the rectangular twin's offset: different
  primitive, different unit, 49 unverifiable surfaces, rule 9.
- Deleting `Colorizer`, which has zero callers: it is the replacement for
  `ColorOverlay`'s 9 live call sites, and now it works.

`tools/check-effect-budget.py` is the gate for 10.11: no `layer.enabled`,
`OpacityMask`, blur or `ShaderEffect` inside a repeated delegate. A `Repeater`'s
whole body counts, a view's does not — `layer.enabled` on a `ListView` is the
recommended fix, not the violation. The shell already had 21 such hits, so it is
shrink-only: `KNOWN` holds them, a new one fails, a fixed one is printed for
removal. It also asserts `StyledDropShadow.samples` never mentions `radius`
again.
