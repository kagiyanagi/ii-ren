refactor(cw-notifications): an expansion that opens as well as it shuts, and a card that answers a pointer

`toggleExpanded()` set `implicitHeightAnim.enabled = false` before expanding and
`true` before collapsing, so a notification group **snapped** open and animated
shut -- the enter/exit rule inverted and then half deleted. Both the card height
and the inner list's spacing also ran on elementMoveFast, the default *effects*
spec, on a size. None of the three is visible to check-design.py: a disabled
Behavior is an absence, and the spatial-on-effects rule only catches the opposite
mistake. One `property AnimSpec expandSpec` now drives both, written inside the
toggle -- elementMove to expand (9's "group expansion on default spatial"),
elementMoveExit to collapse (2.5). Assigning it in the function rather than from
a binding on `expanded` sidesteps 2.9's ordering trap outright, since the write
provably precedes the one that starts the animation.

The brief asked for a deliberate answer on the group's bare MouseArea root. It
stays one. The card has no left-click action -- right-click expands, middle-click
and a swipe dismiss -- 3.2 keeps a ripple off a row that is really a layout and
off any parent of something already rippled, which the expander is, and
SwipeDismissible is itself the MouseArea filling the root, so a RippleButton's
own MouseArea would fight it (10.12). It takes 3.1's second-ranked mechanism
instead: a StateOverlay bound to hover, focus, pressed and dragged, which the
card rendered none of while answering all four. The three pointer states exclude
each other -- stacked, hover plus pressed composites to 0.18, which is the drag
token -- and all four corner radii are explicit, because `clip` is a bounding-box
scissor and does not follow `radius`. 3.6's lift comes from the shadow the popup
already had, now also shown while dragging. `focused` is wired and currently
unreachable; giving the card `activeFocusOnTab` would rewrite the sidebar's tab
order, which is a caller's business.

Two OpacityMasks stay, against 8 and 10.11, and the reasoning is in notes.md
because it is the thing most likely to be "fixed" backwards: Quickshell's
ClippingRectangle is an internal Rectangle with `layer.enabled` plus a
ShaderEffectSource plus a ShaderEffect -- two FBOs and a shader where the mask is
one FBO, and its own documentation says it costs more. StyledFlickable does not
clip, so the actions mask is load-bearing rather than decorative.

Colour, where it was wrong rather than merely untokenised. An action button on a
`colSecondaryContainer` fill painted its label `m3onSurfaceVariant` -- the wrong
pair, and from the generated palette rather than the semantic layer (6.1). It is
onSecondaryContainer/onLayer4 now, through one property that also feeds
colStateLayer, so the focus and pressed films cw-buttons added are films of this
button's content colour and not layer 1's. The expander's resting background was
`ColorUtils.mix(colLayer2, colLayer2Hover, 0.5)`: a hand-mixed tint, which 3.1
forbids, sitting half-way to its own hover so there was almost nothing between
rest and hover. It is a control on a layer-2 card, so it is layer 3.

`NotificationAppIcon.shape` called Math.random() *inside a binding*, so the
urgent shape re-rolled on every re-evaluation and ShapeCanvas morphed the icon
between VerySunny and SoftBurst each time something unrelated changed. Rolled
once. `implicitSize: 38 * scale` multiplied by Item.scale, which MaterialShape
does not define and animating would have resized the icon.

`urgency` is a string everywhere -- Notif.urgency is
`notification?.urgency.toString()`, the enum's decimal -- so the group compared
against `NotificationUrgency.Critical.toString()` while the app icon used a bare
`===` against the enum and was silently never urgent for anything handed the
service's own value. All three read it through Number() now, which covers the
string, the live enum and the JSON-restored form.

Also: both swipe snap-backs asked for elementMove's duration with
expressiveFastSpatial's curve, a spec in neither table; the chevron's rotation is
spatial and was on the effects spec; the expander's contentItem was anchored to
the Control that sizes it (10.14); the inner delegate anchored itself inside its
ListView, which Qt calls undefined; ScrollEdgeFade ramps from an *opaque* copy of
its colour and defaulted to layer 1 over a layer-2 card, so the action row's edge
fade painted a band of the wrong layer. `itemIndex` is handed down from each
delegate rather than read off `parent.children`, `multipleNotifications` had
zero readers, and pragma ComponentBehavior: Bound plus the qualification it needs
took this family's qmllint from 43 warnings to 1.

tools/check-notification-expansion.py holds the part that is invisible when
broken: two different specs assigned to the spec property, at least one spatial,
no Behavior switched off inside the toggle, and no size or rotation Behavior in
the family back on elementMoveFast. Mutation-tested against all six assertions.

Not verified against a running shell, per the parallel-session rules. The
expansion is worth a look at 60fps on a group of three or more -- the inner
ListView lands contentHeight in steps as it builds delegates, which is the most
likely reason someone switched the animation off in the first place.
