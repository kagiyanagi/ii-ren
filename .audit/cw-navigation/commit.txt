refactor(cw-navigation): the film the tabs never had, and a collapse that is not the expand

The brief called this the family with the densest untokenised motion per line.
Mechanically it was four check-design hits in 830 lines; the debt that mattered was
the two shapes no regex reaches. The tab indicator's durations arrive as
`idx1Duration: 50` through the shared AnimatedTabIndexPair, and `\bduration:` does
not match `idx1Duration:` -- the exported-knob blind spot, now its third instance
after CircularProgress and StyledText. And a missing state has no line at all:
check-button-states.py walks QQC2 Button roots, so both TabButton-rooted widgets and
ToolbarTextField sat outside it with no focus layer, SecondaryTabButton with no press
state either once you look past the ripple.

SecondaryTabButton's hover was a hand-rolled 0.05 tint of colOnSurface that a checked
tab switched off entirely. All three states are now one StateOverlay, which the
button's existing OpacityMask already clips to its corners for free -- and its `down`
is driven by hand, because the MouseArea swallows the press and AbstractButton
therefore never set the flag the press reads. RippleButton has always done that; this
one had nothing reading `down` to notice.

Measured rather than argued, with the plain qml runtime and the answer in the exit
code: TabButton already defaults to Qt.StrongFocus and already has activeFocusOnTab,
so the focusPolicy line this started with was dead and is gone; visualFocus does go
true on a tab-reason focus, so the film is reachable and not inert; a `to:` pair of
Transitions fires exactly once each in the right direction; and topLeftRadius follows
radius, so StateOverlay takes one radius instead of four. Also, contrary to the Qt
docs, an inline component does see the enclosing file's root id in 6.11 -- which
RippleButton's own RippleAnim has been relying on all along.

The rail's expand and its collapse stop sharing a spec (2.5): out on the default
spatial one, back on the fast effects one at roughly a quarter the length. The anchor
halves take their direction from a state's `to:`; the width and the chevron have no
state to read, so they use 2.9's assign-from-the-driving-binding shape. The expanded
state's PropertyChanges was restoring the binding the base state already had, which
made the PropertyAnimation beside it a race it usually lost -- qmllint said so twice
and both warnings go with it. NavigationRailTabArray's pill stays symmetric on
elementMoveSmall because it is the selection indicator (9) and one Behavior cannot
tell which cause moved it; its height had no animation at all and simply jumped.

Selection was reading wrong in two places: the rail's label stayed colOnLayer1 on top
of the secondary-container pill while its icon did not, and ToolbarTabButton gave its
content no selected colour whatsoever. Both now crossfade one colContent on
elementMoveFast, which is also the hue their state film should have been.
ToolbarPairedFab stops anchoring its own root -- half its callers are RowLayouts, and
Qt calls that undefined behavior -- stops anchoring its shadow across a Loader
boundary, and drops a clicked(event) that always forwarded undefined.

check-navigation-widgets.py holds the three states on the widgets check-button-states
cannot see, the two named directions and their 2.9 shape, and the indicator durations
that check-design structurally cannot read. 12 mutations, 12 caught.

AnimatedTabIndexPair itself still hand-fits Easing.OutSine on both edges; it lives in
modules/common/models, which no family in this tranche owns, and Workspaces.qml is
its fourth caller. The exact edit is in the notes.
